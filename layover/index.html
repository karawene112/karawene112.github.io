                      How the rule check works
                      • Rest at the outstation: at least 10 hours, or as long as the duty before it if that was longer. It must include an 8-hour sleep opportunity. The app counts arrival to pickup.
                      • Body clock state at pickup: crew stay acclimatised to home time for the first 48 hours. After that, with a time difference of 4 hours or more, the rules treat the body clock as being in an unknown state until enough days pass at the destination (3 to 5 days, depending on the difference).
                      • Rest at home after the trip: after a trip with a time difference of 4 hours or more, 2 to 5 local nights at home are needed before the next trip, depending on the difference and the length of the trip. Switching between an eastbound and a westbound trip of 4+ hours needs at least 3 local nights. A local night is 8 hours between 22:00 and 08:00 home time.
                      • Without your duty hours, the app assumes 10 hours of duty each way. Enter them when saving a layover for a more exact check.

                      Based on the European EASA flight time rules (EU regulation 83/2014: ORO.FTL.105, ORO.FTL.235 and CS FTL.1.235), which apply to cabin crew too. Your country's rules and your company's scheme can differ, and your roster and company rules always come first.

                      How the fatigue points are worked out
                      • Each hour of difference with your home base adds 1 point. Flying east adds 1.5 per hour, because the body adjusts more slowly to it.
                      • A layover under 24 hours adds 2 points, and under 15 hours adds 4.
                      • Landing between midnight and 6am home time adds 3 points.
                      • Starting a layover less than 48 hours after the previous pickup adds 2 points.
                      • A layover is light below 5 points, moderate from 5, heavy from 10. A week is moderate from 10 and heavy from 20.

                      A personal guide to plan rest, not a medical tool or your company's flight time limits. If you feel unfit to fly, follow your company's fatigue reporting process.
